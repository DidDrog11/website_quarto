Reported case fatality ratios (CFRs) are used to set national targets, such as Nigeria's goal of a Lassa fever CFR below 10%, and to plan vaccine and therapeutic trials. They also move with testing, death reporting and where cases occur. Nigeria's reported CFR rose from 18.9% at week 23 of 2025 to 25.0% at the same point in 2026.

This ecological study extracted weekly state-level counts from 474 Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention situation reports (2017–2026). Following a pre-specified plan, it compared years at phase-matched points, decomposed year-on-year change into within-state and between-state components, and estimated the change in ascertainment needed to explain 2026.

By week 36, 70 deaths had been added to the cumulative totals outside any week's figures, so the published CFR exceeded 2025 by 5.4 to 6.2 percentage points. On the weekly series 2026 did not differ from 2025 (+0.3 points, 95% CI −5.0 to 7.6). The rise cannot be attributed to care, and reported CFRs alone cannot track targets or set trial baselines.
