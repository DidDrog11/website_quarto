Risk maps for Lassa fever mostly assume its rodent reservoir, the natal multimammate mouse *Mastomys natalensis*, keeps to the countryside. West African cities are growing fast, and that assumption had not been tested where it matters.

This paper rebuilds the map of where the reservoir can live, using a model that lets several rodent species share or contest the same ground. Once the invasive black rat and house mouse are in the model, *M. natalensis* turns out to tolerate peri-urban land well. The biological hazard reaches the edge of the city.

Spillover does not follow it in. Dense urban infrastructure, tracked with night-time lights, acts as a shield that stops hazard becoming infection. Correcting for that shield and for waning antibodies gives about 2.6 million infections a year across the region, and picks out districts in Nigeria, Benin and Togo that should be reporting cases and report none.
