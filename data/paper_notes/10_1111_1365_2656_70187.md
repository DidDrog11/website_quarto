The natal multimammate mouse, *Mastomys natalensis*, carries Lassa virus, and how many of them live near people depends on the landscape people make. This study trapped small mammals across forest, farm and village sites in eastern Sierra Leone from 2020 to 2023.

*M. natalensis* becomes more common along the gradient from forest to agriculture to village. In peri-urban villages it drops away again, and the house mouse looks to be part of the reason. Where the invasive *Mus musculus* is present, the multimammate mouse is less likely to be. The black rat shows no such effect in rural villages.

Land use and competition between species shape where the reservoir lives, not habitat alone. Predicting Lassa fever risk means counting the whole small-mammal community, above all where land use is changing quickly.
