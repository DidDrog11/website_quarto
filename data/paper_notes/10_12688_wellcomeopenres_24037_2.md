Arenaviruses and hantaviruses are hosted primarily by rodents and shrews and cause substantial human morbidity, yet their global distribution is known only through a literature scattered across decades, languages and disciplines. Without a synthesis, sampling effort cannot be separated from pathogen occurrence.

This protocol specifies how Project ArHa assembles that synthesis: the search strategy across bibliographic databases, screening and inclusion criteria, extraction fields, and a relational structure of five tables covering citations, study design, host occurrence, pathogen assay and genetic sequence. Critically, non-detections are retained, so absence of virus in a sampled host is recorded rather than lost.

The resulting database is spatially and temporally explicit, which makes it usable for quantifying surveillance bias as well as for modelling host-pathogen association. An accompanying Shiny application opens the data to users who do not code. The protocol was published before extraction completed, fixing the method in advance.
